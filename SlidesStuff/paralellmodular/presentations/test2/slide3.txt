Let's explore how Pangea came to be. Approximately 335 million years ago, during the Carboniferous period, Pangea began to assemble. It wasn't formed from nothing—rather, it came together from earlier continental units that had previously existed separately. These included Gondwana, which made up the southern portion, Euramerica in the north, and Siberia. The process of collision and fusion created a massive connected landmass that remained fully assembled for about 100 million years. This was a remarkable period of geological stability before the forces that would eventually tear it apart began to take hold. It's important to note that Pangea wasn't the first supercontinent—others like Rodinia and Pannotia preceded it—but it is the most recent and the one we know the most about.
