Perhaps the most exciting aspect of Pangea was the life it supported. Dinosaurs and other prehistoric animals roamed freely across this vast connected landmass. Because there were no physical barriers like oceans or mountain ranges to prevent movement, flora and fauna could distribute themselves uniformly across the entire continent. This is why we find identical fossil species across continents that are now separated by thousands of miles of ocean. Fossil evidence shows that the same species of plants and animals lived in South America, Africa, and Antarctica—proving they were once connected. During the Triassic and Jurassic periods, Pangea supported rich biodiversity, including early mammals, reptiles, and the dinosaurs that would dominate the Earth for millions of years. This was a time of remarkable evolutionary innovation, made possible by the connected geography of Pangea.
