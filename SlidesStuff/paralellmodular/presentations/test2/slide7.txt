All good things must come to an end, and Pangea was no exception. Around 200 million years ago, at the end of the Triassic period and the beginning of the Jurassic, Pangea began to break apart. The process started with the development of a three-pronged fissure between Africa, South America, and North America. This breakup wasn't random—it was driven by the powerful forces of plate tectonics and mantle convection deep within the Earth. As the continents pulled apart, new ocean basins began to form, most notably the Atlantic Ocean. This wasn't a quick process—rather, the continents gradually drifted apart over tens of millions of years, eventually reaching their current positions. The breakup of Pangea fundamentally changed Earth's geography, climate, and the distribution of life, setting the stage for the world we know today.
