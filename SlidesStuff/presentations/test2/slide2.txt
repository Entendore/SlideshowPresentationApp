So, what exactly was Pangea? The name itself comes from ancient Greek—'pan' meaning 'all' and 'Gaia' meaning 'Earth' or 'land.' Pangea was a supercontinent that existed during the late Paleozoic and early Mesozoic eras, roughly from 299 to 180 million years ago. What's particularly fascinating about its configuration was its C-shape—a massive landmass that stretched all the way from the northern polar regions down to the southern polar regions. This enormous continent was surrounded by a single superocean called Panthalassa, which covered the rest of Earth's surface. Pangea represents the most recent supercontinent in Earth's history, and it was the first to be reconstructed by geologists through careful study of the geological record.
