The geography of Pangea was truly extraordinary. This single, massive landmass was surrounded on all sides by the superocean Panthalassa, which was the largest ocean Earth has ever seen. On the eastern side of Pangea, there were also two additional bodies of water: the Paleo-Tethys Ocean and later the Tethys Ocean. The continent's C-shape meant that it wrapped around these seas, creating a unique geographic configuration. The sheer scale of Pangea is hard to comprehend—it stretched continuously from the North Pole to the South Pole, creating a single connected landmass where animals and plants could migrate freely. This connectedness would have profound implications for life on Earth during this time period. Before its breakup, there were no separate continents as we know them today—just one enormous continent surrounded by water.
